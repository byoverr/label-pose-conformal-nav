\title{Conformal Calibration of Open-Vocabulary Semantic Maps under Label and Pose Uncertainty%
\thanks{Code and data: \url{https://github.com/byoverr/label-pose-conformal-nav}.}}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Sergey Shchetinkin}
\IEEEauthorblockA{ITMO University, Saint Petersburg, Russia}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A robot that avoids ``the plant'' on an open-vocabulary semantic map faces two errors at once: the detector misses,
truncates or mislabels objects, and the drifting pose estimate draws them in the wrong place. Distribution-free
guarantees for planning on perception maps usually calibrate the perception error at a known pose. We calibrate one
per-scene quantity, the \emph{miss distance}: how far the true footprints of a class reach beyond the class region on
the map built with estimated poses, measured in the planner frame. Its split-conformal quantile is a keep-out margin
that certifies every path, whichever planner produced it, and abstains when the margin would be uninformative. On 21
OSMa-Bench scenes, margins calibrated on labels or on pose alone lose coverage under the other error, and the sum of
the two over-pays by up to a quarter. Label-set calibration degenerates to keeping out every occupied cell, because the
detector truncates the edge of every object. The price of the guarantee is set by the worst calibration scene: a single
missed plant makes its class abstain in a quarter to two thirds of the splits. With real visual odometry and no loop closure there is no
useful guarantee.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Open-vocabulary semantic maps~\cite{conceptgraphs} let a robot follow instructions such as ``keep away from the
bike''. The map has two unreliable inputs. A detector or segmenter supplies the labels, and it misses objects,
covers them only in part, or confuses classes. The robot's pose estimate places the observations, and it drifts.
Planners usually take the map at face value and keep a fixed clearance from the regions of avoid classes.

How unsafe this is usually goes unmeasured. OSMa-Bench~\cite{osmabench} shows that open-vocabulary maps degrade
sharply under lighting changes (the f-mIoU of ConceptGraphs drops from 28.00 to 14.29), but it scores segmentation and
scene-graph answers rather than a robot moving through the map. A recent survey of 3D scene graphs~\cite{survey3dsg}
lists the link between map errors and robot behaviour as an open problem.

Conformal prediction (CP)~\cite{gentle} gives a planner guarantees without assumptions on the error distribution.
Sundarsingh et al.~\cite{sundarsingh} calibrate per-cell label sets of a semantic grid, and Perceive With
Confidence~\cite{pwc} calibrates how much to inflate an occupancy map; both assume a known robot pose. Separately
calibrated modules need not form a calibrated system~\cite{baral}.

We ask what guarantee a planner gets when label error and pose error act together, and how to calibrate it.
Contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the \emph{miss distance}, a per-class version of the inflation score of~\cite{pwc} measured in the planner frame
  on a map built with estimated poses; its conformal quantile is a margin that covers both errors for any planner and
  abstains when it would be uninformative;
  \item an analysis of joint versus separate calibration: the sum of two separately calibrated margins guarantees only
  $1 - 2\alpha$, under a premise that geometry does not imply, and we construct a case where it under-covers;
  \item an evaluation on 21 OSMa-Bench scenes with five levels of synthetic drift, real visual odometry, MobileSAM masks
  and a transfer to HM3D, which reports the price of the guarantee (margin, abstention, solved tasks) next to coverage
  and compares the method with label-set calibration and with a margin without semantics.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Open-vocabulary mapping.} ConceptGraphs~\cite{conceptgraphs} fuses class-agnostic masks into an object graph
with CLIP features. OSMa-Bench~\cite{osmabench} and OSMa-Bench++~\cite{osmabenchpp} evaluate such mappers by 3D
segmentation and scene-graph question answering under lighting changes, without a motion task. Naive Bayesian fusion
makes semantic maps over-confident~\cite{overconf}; we fuse by averaging. Tchuiev and Indelman~\cite{tchuiev} keep a
joint Bayesian belief over robot poses, object poses and classes and plan in belief space; the approach is
model-based rather than distribution-free.

\textbf{Conformal guarantees for planning.} Conformal prediction (CP) entered planning through trajectory prediction
of other agents~\cite{lindemann}. Yang et al.~\cite{yang2023} calibrate perception-based state estimation error and
build control barrier functions robust to it, without a map or semantics. Shin et al.~\cite{shin2026} conformalize the
whole distance field to moving obstacles and obtain a constraint that certifies any MPC trajectory; they name folding
perception and state-estimation error into the same pipeline as future work. SCORE~\cite{score} defines the score on a
signed distance field to obtain non-convex keep-out regions. Learnable CP~\cite{learnablecp} learns a context-aware
score for planner clearance and includes synthetic localization noise in its ablations, and planner-agnostic constraint
tightening~\cite{tightening} bounds the deviation between nominal and true dynamics. Closest to us, Sundarsingh et
al.~\cite{sundarsingh} calibrate label sets of grid cells with one score per environment, the worst cell observed along
sampled paths, and plan with the most conservative label of each cell. With a known pose, five classes (including
``free''), $70\times25$~m outdoor sites and a calibration-conditional variant ($\delta = 0.1$), they report 93.36\,\%
correct maps and 98.36\,\% mission success at a 95\,\% target. Perceive With Confidence (PwC)~\cite{pwc} calibrates the
smallest inflation of an occupancy map that covers all obstacles; our miss distance is a per-class version of that score,
measured in the planner frame on a map built with estimated poses.

\textbf{Composition and pose error.} In a synthetic Gaussian example, two modules calibrated at 95\,\% and composed
under an independence assumption give 99.98\,\% at error correlation $-0.9$ and 86.46\,\% at $+0.9$, and estimating the
joint covariance restores the target~\cite{baral}. Calafiore~\cite{calafiore2026} adds blockwise certificates
through a union bound. Mahajan et al.~\cite{mahajan} observe that local conformal
guarantees of occupancy maps degrade because correlation along a trajectory breaks exchangeability, and recover coverage
by fusing maps of several robots; weighting calibration points bounds the coverage loss without
exchangeability~\cite{barber2023}. Pose error in semantic memory is real and poorly calibrated: on a real robot the
nominal 95\,\% ellipsoid of object position derived from the pose graph covers 33\,\% of
re-detections~\cite{pposemem}. A citation and keyword search (October 2026) found no work that calibrates
open-vocabulary label error and robot pose error jointly with a planner in the loop; the search is not exhaustive.

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:problem}

A scene $\Omega$ is drawn from an unknown distribution $\mathcal D$. It contains objects of avoid classes
$\mathcal K$; object $e$ has class $k_e$ and floor footprint $F_e \subset \mathbb R^2$, and
$F_k = \bigcup_{e:\,k_e = k} F_e$. The robot moves along true poses $T_1, \dots, T_Q \in SE(2)$ and receives a task at
time $Q$. Its estimator returns $\hat T_t = E_t T_t$ with accumulated planar error $E_t \in SE(2)$. A grid with cell
size $\Delta$ is fused from all observations placed with the \emph{estimated} poses; cell $j$ stores the fraction
$p_j(k)$ of its observations that support class $k$. The region of class $k$ at threshold $\lambda$ is the set of cell
centres
\begin{equation}
  A_\lambda(k) = \{\, c_j : \text{cell } j \text{ occupied},\ p_j(k) \ge \lambda \,\}.
\end{equation}
The robot believes its pose is $\hat T_Q$, so in the planner frame the true footprint is $\hat F_k = E_Q F_k$. A point
observed at time $t$ is drawn at $E_t x = (E_t E_Q^{-1})\,E_Q x$, i.e., displaced from that truth by the relative error
$E_t E_Q^{-1}$. A motion $G$ common to the whole trajectory (estimates $G E_t T_t$) moves the map and the robot alike
and changes nothing; only relative errors matter.

A path is a sequence of robot-centre positions; it is safe for class $k$ if every point $x$ on it has
$\operatorname{dist}(x, \hat F_k) \ge d$, with $d = 0.5$~m.

\begin{assumption}[execution]\label{as:exec}
The robot follows the path in the planner frame, and the pose error does not change during execution.
\end{assumption}

The guarantee is thus about the plan made at time $Q$, not about closed-loop execution. Given $n$ calibration scenes
with known ground truth, we seek per-class margins $\hat r_k \in [0, \infty]$, as small as possible, such that for a new
scene
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb P\Bigl(\begin{array}{l}\text{every path with } \operatorname{dist}(\gamma, A_\lambda(k)) > d + \hat r_k\\ \text{is safe for class } k\end{array}\Bigr) \ge 1 - \alpha.
  \label{eq:goal}
\end{equation}
The probability is over the calibration scenes, the new scene and the error realizations. The value
$\hat r_k = \infty$ (\emph{abstention}) satisfies~\eqref{eq:goal} trivially by admitting no path.

\begin{assumption}[exchangeability]\label{as:exch}
The pairs $(\Omega_i, \xi_i)$, $i = 1, \dots, n + 1$, of a scene and the realization of perception and pose errors in
it are exchangeable, and the detector, the mapping, $\lambda$ and $R_{\max}$ are fixed before calibration.
\end{assumption}

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\begin{definition}[miss distance]
Let $\hat F_k$ be the centres of the cells hit by points of the true objects of class $k$ moved to the planner frame
(cells beyond the map edge are kept). For a scene and class $k$,
\begin{equation}
  s_k = \min\bigl\{R_{\max},\ h\bigl(\hat F_k, A_\lambda(k)\bigr)\bigr\},\quad
  h(X, Y) = \max_{x\in X} \min_{y\in Y} \|x - y\|,
  \label{eq:miss}
\end{equation}
the directed Hausdorff distance; $s_k = 0$ if the scene has no object of class $k$ and $s_k = R_{\max}$ if the region
is empty.
\end{definition}

A missed or mislabeled object gives a large score, a truncated object the length of the missing part, and a map shift
about its size, with a lever-arm effect for rotations. We compute one score per scene: cells, frames and objects of a
scene are strongly dependent and are not exchangeable units.

\textbf{Calibration.} Let $s^{(1)} \le \dots \le s^{(n)}$ be the sorted calibration scores of class $k$ and
$m = \lceil (n + 1)(1 - \alpha) \rceil$. The margin is $\hat r_k = s^{(m)}$ if $m \le n$ and $s^{(m)} < R_{\max}$, and
$\hat r_k = \infty$ otherwise. With $n = 13$ and $\alpha = 0.1$, $m = 13$: the margin is the largest calibration score.
Calibrating per class keeps one unreliable class from inflating the others.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:valid}
Under Assumptions~\ref{as:exec} and~\ref{as:exch},
$\mathbb P\bigl(\hat r_k = \infty \text{ or } s_k(\Omega_{n+1}) \le \hat r_k\bigr) \ge m/(n + 1) \ge 1 - \alpha$, and
$\hat r_k$ satisfies~\eqref{eq:goal} for paths through cell centres.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By exchangeability the rank of $s_k(\Omega_{n+1})$ among the $n + 1$ scores is uniform, and ties only help, so
$\mathbb P(s_k(\Omega_{n+1}) \le s^{(m)}) \ge m/(n + 1)$~\cite{gentle}; under abstention the event holds. If
$\hat r_k < \infty$ and $s_k \le \hat r_k$, every $y \in \hat F_k$ has some $a \in A_\lambda(k)$ with
$\|y - a\| \le \hat r_k$. For a path point $x$ with $\operatorname{dist}(x, A_\lambda(k)) > d + \hat r_k$, the triangle
inequality gives $\|x - y\| \ge \|x - a\| - \|a - y\| > d$.
\end{proof}

The certificate does not depend on the planner, which only decides whether a path exists in the narrowed free space.
For continuous footprints and motion between cell centres the distance can fall short of $d$ by at most
$\sqrt 2\,\Delta \approx 7$~cm. The guarantee is per class, so with mission classes $\mathcal K_m$ the union bound gives
$1 - |\mathcal K_m|\,\alpha$ for all of them. It is marginal over calibration sets: for $n = 13$ the coverage of a fixed
calibration set follows $\mathrm{Beta}(13, 1)$, with mean 0.93 and 10th percentile 0.84.

\textbf{Compared calibrations.} \emph{Uncalibrated}: region $A_{\lambda_0}(k)$, margin 0. \emph{Labels only}: the
score~\eqref{eq:miss} of the detector map at true poses, margin $\hat r^{\mathrm{lab}}_k$. \emph{Pose only}: the score
of a ground-truth-label map built with drifted poses, $\hat r^{\mathrm{pose}}_k$. \emph{Sum}:
$\hat r^{\mathrm{lab}}_k + \hat r^{\mathrm{pose}}_k$, both at level $\alpha$. \emph{Joint} (ours): the score of the
detector map built with drifted poses. These five share the region $A_{\lambda_0}(k)$, $\lambda_0 = 0.05$, chosen on a
development scene. \emph{Geometry only}: every occupied cell may be the class ($\lambda = 0$), with a margin calibrated on
the drifted map; this is the PwC-style certificate without semantics. \emph{Label sets}~\cite{sundarsingh}: the score is
the worst $1 - p_j(k)$ over true footprint cells ($\infty$ if a cell is unoccupied), $\hat\eta$ is its conformal
quantile, and cells with $p_j(k) \ge 1 - \hat\eta$ are avoided, with no metric margin. Their score takes the maximum over
all observed cells and classes; restricting it to footprint cells can only lower it. Arms without a margin count as
covering a miss of up to one cell, which favours them.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:sep}
If $s_k \le s^{\mathrm{lab}}_k + s^{\mathrm{pose}}_k$ for the scores of the new scene, then
$\mathbb P(s_k \le \hat r^{\mathrm{lab}}_k + \hat r^{\mathrm{pose}}_k) \ge 1 - 2\alpha$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each margin fails to cover its own score with probability at most $\alpha$; by the union bound both cover with
probability at least $1 - 2\alpha$, and then the premise gives the claim.
\end{proof}

The premise does not follow from geometry. The triangle inequality gives
$s_k \le s^{\mathrm{lab}}_k + h(E_Q A^0, A)$, where $A^0$ is the detector region at true poses: the second term is the
displacement of the \emph{predicted} region, while the pose-only score measures a ground-truth-label map, as an engineer
tuning localization separately from perception would. In our data the premise fails in a fraction of realizations
(Sec.~\ref{sec:composition}). A budget of $\alpha/2$ per module~\cite{calafiore2026} restores $1 - \alpha$ but needs
$n \ge 2/\alpha - 1 = 19$ at $\alpha = 0.1$. The joint margin does not depend on the unknown error dependence.

\textbf{Missions.} A robot of radius 0.2~m must reach a goal. The keep-out zone of class $k$ is the set of cell centres
within $d + \hat r_k$ of its region; a class that abstains falls back to the geometry-only certificate. Dijkstra's
algorithm on the 8-connected grid is complete on the grid graph, so ``no path'' means that none exists. A path is
\emph{unsafe} if one of its cell centres is closer than $d$ to a cell of a true avoid object in the planner frame. It
\emph{collides} if it comes closer than $0.2\,\mathrm{m} - \Delta$ to a true obstacle (the obstacle map is not
calibrated, and a one-cell shift is not counted as a crash). A mission \emph{succeeds} if a path is found, is safe and
does not collide. A shortest path tests the certificate along one line only, so we also record the worst case over all
planners: whether a cell closer than $d$ to a true object is reachable from the start without entering the keep-out
zone. The same planner on the true map is an oracle.

\textbf{Hypotheses.} With $\mathrm{Cov}(r)$ the left side of~\eqref{eq:goal}: a labels-only margin has
$\mathrm{Cov} < 1 - \alpha$ once drift is comparable to the label error (H1); a pose-only margin has
$\mathrm{Cov} < 1 - \alpha$ while drift is smaller than the label error (H2); the joint margin is no larger than the sum,
$\hat r^{\mathrm{joint}}_k \le \hat r^{\mathrm{lab}}_k + \hat r^{\mathrm{pose}}_k$ (H3); map mIoU is a poor predictor of
mission safety (H4). The coverage of the joint margin is not a hypothesis: it follows from
Proposition~\ref{prop:valid}, and measuring it checks the implementation.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\textbf{Data and perception.} We use 22 ReplicaCAD scenes of OSMa-Bench~\cite{osmabench,habitat2} with RGB-D,
ground-truth semantics and poses at $640\times480$, every 8th frame. All design choices were made on \texttt{apt\_0},
which is never used for calibration or testing. YOLO-World v2-s~\cite{yoloworld} runs with the 98 object classes of the
dataset as prompts; inside each box we keep pixels within 0.25~m of the median box depth. Maps are 5~cm grids, and points
0.1--1.8~m above the floor are obstacles. The avoid classes are indoor plant, bike and TV stand; a true footprint is a
connected component of at least 8 occupied cells with at least two points of the class.

\textbf{Pose error.} Synthetic drift replays every relative motion of the true trajectory with planar noise
proportional to the distance travelled and the angle turned. Besides the true pose (L0), five levels are set on the
development scene, L1--L3 by absolute trajectory error (ATE) and L4--L5 by endpoint drift. On the evaluation scenes the
median ATE is 0.4~cm (L1), 2~cm (L2), 7~cm (L3), 45~cm (L4) and 2.7~m (L5); we draw 10 realizations per level. As real
pose error we run frame-to-frame RGB-D odometry of Open3D~\cite{open3d,steinbrucker} on every 2nd frame with a planar
constraint and no loop closure, without tuning it on ground truth.

\textbf{Coverage protocol.} $\alpha = 0.1$ unless stated, $R_{\max} = 3$~m. The 21 evaluation scenes are split 200
times at random into 13 calibration and 8 test scenes (all splits are enumerated when there are at most 2000). A
calibration scene contributes one random drift realization, a test scene all of them. Coverage is the fraction of test
realizations whose score is within the margin, averaged over splits, with abstention counted as covered as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:valid}; we also report the share of splits with a certificate and the coverage among them. Because
splits are drawn from one fixed set of scenes, the scenes are exchangeable by construction and the joint coverage is at
least $1 - \alpha$ on average for any data (up to the random choice of realizations). It is a check of the implementation; the margin, the abstention rate and the behaviour of
the arms without a guarantee are what the experiment measures.

\textbf{Tasks.} Start and goal are at least 1.5~m from avoid objects and 3~m apart. \emph{Hard} tasks are selected so
that the shortest obstacle-only path passes within 0.5~m of a plant or bike (233 tasks in 15 scenes); \emph{random} tasks
are not selected (20 per scene, 420 in total). Margins for a scene are calibrated on the other 20 scenes ($m = 19$). The
mission avoid classes are plant and bike. Both the task family and this choice were made after first runs: an earlier
task family with goals near the objects was degenerate, and the TV stand was removed after it turned out to abstain. The
coverage guarantee does not depend on these choices, but mission rates should be read with them in mind.

The uncalibrated map almost never contains the whole true footprint, because the detector truncates object edges
(Table~\ref{tab:coverage}). Label sets reach 1.00 at the true pose only because their threshold $1 - \hat\eta$ is zero:
in all 21 scenes every class has an occupied footprint cell with no observation of that class (a truncated edge), so
the score is 1 and every occupied cell is avoided, exactly the zone of the geometry-only arm at L0. With no metric slack,
2~cm of drift drops their bike coverage to 0.73. The labels-only margin holds while drift stays below the label error
and falls to 0.76 for the bike at 45~cm, with a 90\,\% scene-bootstrap interval of 0.67--0.82 (H1); for the plant the
drop is smaller (0.86) and its interval, 0.59--0.90, only touches the target. The pose-only margin cannot see detector errors and stays at or below 0.27 up to
7~cm (H2). The sum over-pays: at 45~cm the joint margin is 23--25\,\% smaller (1.23 vs.\ 1.61~m for the bike, 1.54
vs.\ 2.06~m for the plant), and 6--25\,\% smaller at 0.4--7~cm (H3).

For plant and bike the joint margin holds 0.92--0.94 at L0--L4, as it must by construction. Its price and abstention
are what matter. The bike is certified at every level up to L4 and its margin grows from 0.75 to 1.23~m. The plant is
missed entirely in one of the 21 scenes; with 13 calibration scenes the margin is the maximum, so at L0 the class
abstains whenever that scene is in the calibration set (63\,\% of the splits, about 13/21); under drift that scene's
miss is sometimes below the cap and abstention is 24--59\,\%. When a certificate is issued, the worst scenes tend to be
in the test set and the coverage among certified splits is 0.81--0.92, below the target, which a marginal guarantee
allows. The TV stand, confidently labeled as a bench, rug or bed in three scenes, abstains in 75--95\,\% of the splits. Both mission classes are covered at once in
85--87\,\% of test realizations, consistent with the union bound $1 - 2\alpha$.

On hard tasks the uncalibrated planner almost always finds a path, but 54--58\,\% of its paths are unsafe at L0--L3: it
grazes the truncated edge of the object (Fig.~\ref{fig:example}, Table~\ref{tab:missions}). The joint, sum and

pose, but 9 and 27\,\% of their paths are unsafe at 2 and 7~cm of drift. The price is that a path exists in only
30--32\,\% of these deliberately hard tasks, against 97--100\,\% on the true map, so the uncalibrated planner has a
higher success rate (39--45\,\% vs.\ 29--32\,\%): about half of its paths are safe by luck. The guarantee bounds risk;
it does not raise success. Map mIoU does not predict this risk: across 15 scenes its Spearman correlation with
the uncalibrated planner's share of unsafe paths is 0.18 (95\,\% bootstrap interval $-0.35$ to $0.68$), too few
scenes to establish either a relation or its absence (H4). Semantics pays off: the geometry-only arm finds a path in 27\,\% of the hard tasks at L0
and in 5--15\,\% under drift, 2--6 times less often than the joint margin. On random tasks the joint margin succeeds in
78\,\% of the missions at the true pose and in 68\,\% at 7~cm, with no unsafe path; the uncalibrated planner succeeds
in 93 and 80\,\%, but 7--8\,\% of its paths are unsafe. Collisions with obstacles, which the guarantee does not cover,
reach 3--5\,\% at 7~cm of drift for the arms whose paths follow walls. With the TV stand as a third avoid class, it
falls back to the geometry-only certificate and the joint arm finds a path in only 6--23\,\% of the hard tasks.

setting (0.95, known pose) the joint margin coincides with them (0.42): with 20 calibration scenes and $\alpha = 0.05$

true pose the vocabulary does not matter and the risk level makes the difference: at $1 - \alpha = 0.9$ the joint
margin succeeds in 78\,\% of the missions against 43\,\% with either vocabulary. Under drift at $\alpha = 0.05$ the
closed vocabulary helps the joint margin (0.61 and 0.50 vs.\ 0.46 and 0.34), because the plant abstains less often. Under drift label sets admit unsafe paths (0.4\,\% of the
missions at 2~cm and 1.1\,\% at 7~cm; a dangerous cell is reachable in 56--60\,\% of realizations), while the joint
margin has none and succeeds more often: 0.46--0.74 at 2~cm and 0.34--0.68 at 7~cm against 0.39 and 0.31. Our absolute success is below the 98.36\,\% reported by~\cite{sundarsingh} (whose most-likely-label baseline
reaches 65.57\,\%), but their method gives 43\,\% on our scenes, so the gap comes from the setting (open sites, a
1~m grid, exploration) rather than the method; likewise PwC reports 20\,\% collisions and 53.3\,\% goals reached on
hardware against 66.7 and 33.3\,\% for its baseline~\cite{pwc}, and its geometry-only analogue reaches 43\,\% on our
random tasks against 78\,\% for the joint margin. We repeated
both calibrations at the true pose on grids of 5, 25, 50 and 100~cm: the label-set threshold is zero at every resolution
and its keep-out zone takes 64\,\% of the free space at 5~cm and all of it at 1~m, while in this run ($\alpha = 0.05$, calibration on
the other 20 scenes) the joint margin abstains for the plant at every resolution and for the bike from 50~cm. A label set can only say ``this cell may be a plant''; a distance says ``a
plant is nearby'', which stays informative when the detector loses the edges of objects.

\subsection{The price of the guarantee}

Raising the risk level barely helps on hard tasks. From $\alpha = 0.1$ to $0.3$ the joint margin at L3 shrinks by
0.20--0.25~m and the share of hard tasks with a path goes from 30 to 31\,\%, while coverage tracks $1 - \alpha$ and the
share of realizations with a reachable dangerous cell rises from 3 to 20--27\,\%. At $\alpha = 0.05$ the margin over 20
scenes is the largest miss, a class falls back in 73\,\% of realizations, and a path exists in 10\,\% of the hard tasks.
On random tasks at 7~cm the picture is the same: 34\,\% mission success at $\alpha = 0.05$, 68\,\% at 0.1 and
71\,\% at 0.3.
The margin is set by the worst calibration scene while $n < 2/\alpha - 1$. With 19 calibration scenes the rank is
$n - 1$, and one scene with a missed plant no longer forces abstention: the plant abstains in 0\,\% of the splits at L0
instead of 63\,\% and in 11\,\% at 45~cm instead of 59\,\%, and the bike margin at 45~cm drops from 1.23 to 1.00~m.
Better masks help only in part. MobileSAM~\cite{mobilesam} prompted with the detector boxes cuts the median bike miss
at L0 from 0.43 to 0.05~m, but the worst scene keeps 0.75~m, so the bike margin at L0 does not change; at 45~cm it drops
from 1.23 to 1.06~m. The plant margin drops from 0.85 to 0.61~m at L0 but grows from 1.54 to 1.86~m at 45~cm. The region
threshold $\lambda_0 = 0.05$ was set on the development scene; $\lambda_0 = 0.02$ gives slightly smaller margins (bike
at 45~cm: 1.08 vs.\ 1.23~m), while 0.1 and 0.2 make the plant abstain in 65--100\,\% of the splits.

\textbf{What lowers the price.} The plant's worst miss comes from two small pots in one scene: the main detector
takes one for a wall cabinet and does not see the other. YOLO-World v2-x and YOLOE-v8-S~\cite{yoloe}, with the same 98
prompts, both find the plant: its worst miss drops from 3 to 0.71--0.72~m, plant abstention nearly vanishes and the
L0 margin drops from 0.85 to 0.71--0.72~m. But each detector has its own whole-object miss. v2-x confuses the TV stand
even more often and marks plants and bikes where there are none (their regions grow by 32 and 10\,\%), so at 7~cm of
drift a path exists in 23\,\% of the hard tasks instead of 30\,\%. YOLOE swaps the TV stand in one scene instead of
three but loses the bike entirely in two, so the bike abstains in 83--85\,\% of the splits and a path exists in only
7--16\,\% of the hard tasks. The bottleneck moves between classes rather than disappearing. The TV stand is swapped
because of competitors in the vocabulary: the closed five-class vocabulary certifies it in every split, but the
detector then calls benches and beds TV stands and three-class missions get no easier (5--8\,\% vs.\ 6--23\,\%);
merging a class with its usual ``twins'' cannot be done honestly, because on the development scene the TV stand is
confused with a screen and a monitor, not with the bench, rug and bed of the test scenes. Growing the class region into
adjacent occupied cells by 15--30~cm barely changes the margin (by at most 0.14~m) while the region grows 2--4 times:
the worst misses are whole objects, not truncated edges. Hierarchical conformal calibration~\cite{hcp} over all 10
drift realizations of each calibration scene removes the lottery of drawing one (the spread of the bike margin at
45~cm falls from 0.49 to 0.18~m) but raises its median (1.24 to 1.50~m), and one margin for both classes covers both
in 92--94\,\% of the realizations instead of 88--92\,\% at a 0.03--0.26~m larger mean margin. A conditional margin
would need a feature that predicts the miss; path length does not (Spearman $-0.09$ to $0.15$), and a whole-object
miss leaves no trace on the map for any feature to see.

\textbf{Risk instead of abstention.} A path with clearance $c_k$ to the region of class $k$ can be unsafe for $k$ only if
$s_k > c_k$, so $\rho_k = (1 + \#\{i: s_{i,k} \ge \min(c_k, R_{\max})\})/(n + 1)$ over the calibration scores is a conformal
p-value: $\mathbb P(\text{unsafe and } \rho_k \le a) \le a$ even for a path chosen on the test map. Planning with a grid
of margins and certifying the first path with $\rho_k \le a/2$ for both classes gives, at $a = 0.2$, a path for
27--29\,\% of the hard and 70--77\,\% of the random tasks --- the same as the joint margin at $\alpha = 0.1$ --- with at
most 5.7\,\% of the certified paths unsafe. It does not raise feasibility, but it replaces abstention by an explicit
risk and explains it: with 20 calibration scenes the smallest risk per class is 1/21, and the scene with the missed
plant raises it to 2/21.

\subsection{When does the sum of margins under-cover?}\label{sec:composition}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{composition_stress_a03_L5.png}
\caption{Coverage of the sum of separate margins and of the joint margin vs.\ the fraction of scenes with a
localization failure placed where labels are worst (dashed), best (solid) or at random (dotted); $1 - \alpha = 0.7$.}
\label{fig:composition}
\end{figure}

On synthetic drift, which is independent of detector errors, the sum only over-pays, although the premise of
Proposition~\ref{prop:sep} fails in 2--12\,\% of plant and bike realizations at L1--L4 and up to 26\,\% at L5. To see the sum
under-cover we keep the real label errors and replace drift by a scene-level mixture: in $b$ of 21 scenes a localization
failure occurs (realizations from L5), in the others drift is negligible (L1). Failures are placed in the scenes with the
worst labels, the best labels, or at random; $\alpha = 0.3$, because at $\alpha = 0.1$ with 13 scenes any failure
enters the maximum, and 1000 splits. When failures coincide with bad labels the sum over-pays (0.74--0.82); when they
hit scenes with good labels it falls to 0.68--0.69 at a 0.70 target (14--24\,\% failing scenes), and to 0.66 at random.
The joint margin gives 0.69--0.74 however failures are placed, close to the rank bound $10/14 \approx 0.71$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:composition}). The under-coverage is small; it shows that the $1 - 2\alpha$ bound is not a formality and
that where the sum lands depends on how failures are placed, which is unknown in advance.

\subsection{Real odometry and other scenes}

Frame-to-frame odometry without loop closure errs in jumps, mostly during in-place turns near walls: its ATE ranges from
3.5~cm to 3.9~m across scenes, with a median of 78~cm. The labels-only margin then silently covers only 0.62 (bike) and
0.74 (plant). The joint margin is formally valid (0.96), but only because it abstains in 87\,\% of the splits for both
classes; when it certifies, coverage is 0.71--0.72. In missions it finds no path: both classes fall back to the
geometry-only certificate, which closes almost all free space at this error. The uncalibrated planner finds a path in
23\,\% of the hard tasks, and 62\,\% of those paths are unsafe. With this pose quality there is no useful guarantee.
Margins calibrated on ReplicaCAD and tested on HM3D scenes~\cite{hm3d} of OSMa-Bench give one counterexample: of the
two scenes in which a plant is visible from the trajectory, the detector finds one (miss 0.18~m) and splits the other
across three wrong classes (2.9~m). A milder shift, a held-out layout of the same family (four groups of five scenes and
\texttt{apt\_3}), keeps the mean joint coverage at 0.94--0.96, but single layouts drop to 0.80 at L0 and 0.63
at 45~cm (bike in the single \texttt{apt\_3} scene).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

The joint margin is not cheap: its price is set by the worst calibration scene, and a single object missed by the
detector makes its class abstain in a large share of the splits. More calibration scenes weaken this dependence:
with 19 of them the plant abstains in 0--11\,\% of the splits instead of 24--63\,\%. Three expectations were wrong. Positive correlation between the errors makes the sum of margins more conservative;
under-coverage appears when rare failures of the two modules hit different scenes, and it is small. Label sets
degenerate because of truncated edges, not complete misses. And real odometry errs in jumps rather than smooth drift.
In earlier runs (not repeated) neither convex hulls of the regions nor a confidence-scaled margin shrank the
keep-out zone. The bottleneck is confident misses and class swaps of whole objects, which map metrics such as mIoU do not show; a
``fraction of scenes with a whole-object miss'' metric would fit mapping benchmarks.

Limitations. One family of 21 simulated scenes and one lighting condition; coverage on random splits of these scenes
holds by construction, and only bootstrap intervals 0.1--0.15 wide, a held-out layout and two HM3D scenes speak to new
scenes. Drift is synthetic and independent of detector errors; real odometry is tested only without loop closure. The
perception is a box detector, not a full mapper. The guarantee is marginal over calibration sets, per class, and about
the plan in the planner frame at query time: execution-time localization error and collisions with obstacles are not
covered, and discretization can take up to 7~cm of the safety distance. The hard task family and the mission classes
were chosen after first runs, and label sets are our re-implementation on final maps, without the authors' exploration
mode.

\section{Conclusion}

The miss distance in the planner frame is one calibrated quantity that gives a planner a distribution-free guarantee
against label and pose errors together, and abstains when the guarantee would be useless. Margins calibrated on either
error alone lose coverage under the other, the sum of separate margins over-pays or under-covers depending on an unknown
placement of failures, and label-set calibration degenerates to keeping out everything occupied. The price is set by
the worst perception error among the calibration scenes, which points to more calibration scenes, SLAM with loop
closures, full open-vocabulary mappers and execution-time pose error as next steps.
